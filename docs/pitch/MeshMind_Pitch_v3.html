v2 said: "Next proof: true three-phone A → B → C multi-hop" and "AI routing advantage: target only, until benchmarked." Both have moved. This edition raises the ambition — and keeps the same discipline about what is proven.

OnePlus 12R → Realme RMX5108 → Galaxy S24 Ultra over Nearby Connections. 11 public human-contact traces, 145 independent episodes, 1,001,442 routing decisions labelled by a temporal oracle. The most important lab finding was a failure.

Our first SAGE pilot "lost" to flooding: 3.3% vs 5.2% delivery. Instead of tuning the model, we measured the benchmark. Even a router with perfect knowledge of the future could only reach 5–12% in that setup — flooding was already at the ceiling. No model could have won. We rebuilt the benchmark so that a perfect router reaches 33–69%, added delivery-ACK gossip, shared radio airtime and real crowd traces, then retrained.

The lesson is the moat: we test whether a test can be won before we celebrate or panic about a result. Investors and organisers get numbers with their ceilings attached.

MeshMind01 / The pitch in 90 seconds
01 The pitch in 90 seconds

20,000 phones.
One metre apart.
Now they think together.

A dense crowd is the largest computer most people will ever stand inside: thousands of radios, accelerometers, microphones, barometers and batteries within arm's reach. Today each phone fights alone for a congested tower. When the tower fails, the event goes blind — exactly when someone needs help and someone else needs to take responsibility for helping.

Product definition · v3
MeshMind is the nervous system of a crowd: a mesh that routes itself, phones that sense together, and a response loop that does not stop at "delivered" — it stops at "resolved".
01 · Nerves

The mesh

Offline transport, store-and-forward, priority pre-emption, Internet through any consenting phone.

02 · Reflexes

SAGE autonomic routing

The network decides who carries what, what goes first, what waits — and heals its own dead zones.

03 · Senses

Crowd Pulse

Consented on-device sensing: density trends, motion coherence, falls, floors — evidence, never verdicts.

04 · Judgement

The response loop

Living Incidents, the one right question, a forecast of where the event is about to go blind.

What MeshMind will never be

  • A system where a model can demote, delay or suppress an explicit SOS — tested adversarially
  • A crush-prediction product. Crowd sensing is evidence for trained humans
  • A certified life-safety system — it is decision support for trained teams
  • An Internet proxy: gateways run allowlisted requests only

Why staff first, attendees second

30 equipped stewards make a backbone on day one; attendees opt in through Event Mode and extend its reach. Every attendee phone that joins makes the network denser, the senses sharper and the model smarter — the crowd is both the user and the infrastructure.

"Nerves → reflexes → senses → judgement" is the build order. Each layer ships only when the layer below is measured.03

MeshMind04 / The engineering discovery
04 The most valuable bug we found

"Sent" is not "delivered."
We measured the difference.

Nearby's send-completion callback is not an application-level delivery signal. Chunks were reported as sent 1.3–2.9 s before the receiver had them; a large transfer silently piled up inside the transport and the first SOS behind it waited 2,374 ms.

SOS latency during a bulk transfer
Before: transport-level "sent"
2,374 ms
After: receiver-confirmed backpressure
145 ms
16×
faster critical delivery under load
The fix
Every chunk gets an application-level confirmation. A sender allows at most two unconfirmed chunks per link — urgent packets bypass that window.
sender.window = 2 // unconfirmed chunks / link on chunk_ack(id) → window.release(id) on enqueue(p): if p.priority == CRITICAL send_now(p) // bypass window else wait_for(window)
Fix 01

Connection recovery

STATUS_ENDPOINT_IO_ERROR could strand two devices. Retry now backs off with jitter.

Fix 02

Lifecycle

Leaving the screen could stop a transfer. Sending now continues independently, with a regression test.

Fix 03

Three phone makers

OnePlus, Realme and Samsung interoperate on one mesh — the mixed-fleet reality of an Indian crowd.

This is the moat in miniature: competitors can copy a feature list. They cannot copy months of measured radio behaviour on real, mixed Android devices — or a simulator calibrated against it.

MeshMind04 / Two hours on three phones
04 The physical stress session

342 attempts. Two hops.
The SOS never lost the race.

We drove three phones from three makers through text, offline Internet, SOS under bulk load, deliberate outages and relay crashes — automatically, for two hours, keeping every failure. An emergency packet had to cross a relay busy pushing up to a megabyte of photos. Every SOS that arrived, arrived first.

SOS round trip vs queued bulk data · 2 hops · ms

Eight times more queued data (8 → 64 chunks) moved the median SOS by only 58 ms. Priority pre-emption plus receiver-confirmed backpressure keep the emergency lane open at both hops.

Survives a dead relay

We killed the relay's app with a packet queued. On restart it reloaded the queue from its database and forwarded it — 9 of 10 times, median 21.7 s. A phone dying mid-crowd no longer loses the message.

Survives an absent receiver

Destination offline for 5–30 s: the relay held every packet and delivered all 40 when it returned. Store-and-forward now works across two hops, not one.

What broke — and why it matters

Cold discovery of the full chain took ~70 s (5/8 succeeded). A warm restart after hours of bulk traffic crashed C while loading its queue — out of memory at a 256 MiB heap, a bug only a long physical run could surface. Both are on the fix list.

Every run was audited: matching relay receive/send logs, a two-hop route, reverse ACK evidence. Of 7 failures, 4 are MeshMind's and 3 the test harness's (ADB drop-outs, an operator stop) — all kept in the report, none replaced.

MeshMind05 / SAGE · what the lab proved
05 SAGE · Semantic-Aware Gateway & Encounter routing

Flooding sends everything.
SAGE knows which copies matter.

At every encounter a phone must decide: does giving this neighbour a copy actually help? We replayed real human movement and asked a time-travelling oracle for the true answer a million times — then trained a model that must answer using only the past.

1 · Real crowds

11 public traces: conferences, a 69-day science exhibition, schools, offices, a hospital, 28 days of campus smartphone Bluetooth

2 · Replay

Synthetic MeshMind traffic, ACK gossip, shared airtime, finite buffers, gateways, churn

3 · Oracle

Exact earliest-arrival over the future trace labels each decision — verified against exhaustive search

4 · Learn

59 history-only features → 15k-parameter siamese network on one RTX 3050 in two minutes

5 · Prove

Held-out events, people and days never seen in training or tuning

Spotting a copy that will change the outcome · held-out test, ROC AUC

Why it works

The gain head sees both phones and their relationship. Two friends who walk together add little by copying to each other; a stranger heading for the exit adds a lot. Comparing the two phones' values alone scores only 0.59 — redundancy is the signal hand-written rules miss.

0.84

41–43%
of useful copies found in the top 10% of offers (crowd, campus)
Routing result on held-out events · the honest version

484 paired runs on 22 unseen episodes. Max-delivery SAGE beat bounded flooding in all 17 stress scenarios: 40.8% vs 40.3% delivery (+0.54 pts, 95% CI +0.44 to +0.65) at the same airtime, reaching 94.7% of the perfect-knowledge ceiling. Eco SAGE matched flooding's delivery with 28% less airtime (−0.11 pts, not significant), 28% fewer transmissions and a 191 s lower p95 latency. At low budgets it ties Spray-and-Focus. SOS outcomes were identical in every run. Gains are real but small; the airtime saving is the practical win.

AUC = probability a truly useful copy is ranked above a useless one; 0.5 is chance. Test split: SFHH conference, the last 6 days of the Copenhagen study, the last 14 days of the Infectious exhibition. CC BY-NC-SA / CC0 / MIT data licences apply.09
MeshMind05 / SAGE · the headroom
05 What perfect foresight would do

SAGE already routes like
perfect foresight. The next prize
is a quarter of the battery.

We swapped the model for a cheater that sees the future — it knows exactly which copies will matter. That is the best any routing model could ever do. The result tells us where the remaining value is: not in delivering more, but in delivering the same with far less radio time.

01 · Delivery: maxed out

SAGE already matches foresight

Sending broadly, a perfect predictor beats flooding by +0.40 pts — and SAGE achieves exactly the same +0.40. Smarter models cannot deliver much more under these conditions.

02 · Efficiency: wide open

72% less data, same delivery

Copying only where it truly helps, perfect foresight matches flooding with 28% of the bytes — and under full buffers even delivers more (+0.6 pts), because useless copies stop crowding out useful ones.

03 · Next: confident skips

Learn when a copy is useless

SAGE eco captures 28% savings today. The gap is confidence at the selective end — so SAGE v2 targets "safe to skip" directly.

SAGE v2 research plan
  • Train on SAGE's own decisions (on-policy relabelling)
  • Labels that account for packet loss and radio contention
  • A calibrated "safe to skip" probability with conformal thresholds
  • Calibrate airtime and contact length from phone telemetry
The pitch line
"SAGE already routes about as well as perfect foresight allows. Our next target is doing it with 3–4× less battery and airtime — the thing that decides whether a mesh survives a whole festival."
Headroom diagnostic: 162 runs on 6 validation Infectious days × {base, heavy load, queue saturation}; "perfect foresight" uses the offline oracle and ignores losses and contention, so real savings will be smaller. SAGE eco's 72% is the 484-run held-out test average.10

MeshMind07 / Mesh Twin
07 Signature · Mesh Twin

See the network's future.
Fix the dead zone before it dies.

The organiser console keeps a live digital twin of the mesh: every phone's last-reported state, queue pressure, gateway health and contact history, replayed forward with the same simulator SAGE was trained in. It answers the question no dashboard can: where will we go blind in the next ten minutes?

01 · Forecast

"North Stand: 71% chance of losing its last gateway route in ~8 min; 23 queued reports would strand."

02 · Explain

Shows why: two relay phones below 15% battery, crowd flow moving toward exits, one gateway flickering.

03 · Pre-connect

Recommends a pre-approved checkpoint for one steward — or dropping a Relay Kit — before the zone goes dark.

04 · Score itself

Every forecast is logged and graded afterwards. The twin publishes its own hit rate and false alarms.

What makes it possible

The pieces already exist in the lab: a validated temporal oracle, a replay simulator, a leakage-tested history state and a learned reachability model (AUC 0.84 for "will this phone deliver?"). The twin points them at live telemetry instead of public traces.

Honesty rules
  • Forecasts are shown as ranges with their own measured accuracy
  • Stale data makes a zone grey, never green
  • Humans approve every movement; recommendations that fail are logged
Status: RESEARCH → BUILDING. The reactive version (Reconnect a Zone) is in the 30-day plan; the predictive twin follows once field telemetry calibrates the simulator. Illustrative visual, not measured data.12

S3 Pre-connect a Zone · the memorable moment

Move one phone.
The event never goes blind.

v2 reacted: when a zone stopped acknowledging, MeshMind recommended where a steward could stand to restore contact. v3 anticipates: the Mesh Twin flags the zone minutes before it drops, and the organiser can approve the move while the backlog is still small.

Relay Kit

A spare Android phone on a power bank, clipped at a checkpoint, turns a recommendation into a permanent fix. Pre-place a handful at known dead spots before doors open. They double as zone beacons for Crowd Pulse.

Sky Relay · vision

For melas and floods: tethered-balloon or mast-mounted relay pods that lift a Relay Kit above the crowd's body-blocking, extending range across ghats and river banks. Pilot only with permits and a safety review.

S6 Swarm Relays · new in v3

Idle phones volunteer to heal the mesh.

When SAGE sees a bridge forming — a phone with high battery, a charger, and contact with two otherwise separate groups — it can ask that attendee, once, to keep MeshMind awake as a relay for the next 30 minutes. Consent is explicit, the battery cost is shown up front, and the reward is visible: "you carried 41 messages tonight, including 2 SOS". Staff phones are always relays; attendee relays are a gift, never an assumption.

  • Asked only where the twin predicts real value
  • Battery floor and time limit chosen by the user
  • Relay credits: festival perks, recognition, priority rendezvous
  • Never extends what a relay can read: relays forward ciphertext

MeshMind09 / Signature S5 · SOS for people who cannot type
S5 Silent SOS + Bhasha SOS

No typing. No speaking.
Still heard.

In a crush you cannot raise your arms; in panic you cannot type; in a crowd of twelve languages the medic may not read yours. MeshMind gives the SOS three entrances — and every one of them leaves on the deterministic critical path within milliseconds, before any AI enrichment.

Entrance 1 · Pattern

Press, don't look

Five presses of the power button or a held volume-key pattern sends a basic SOS with zone and time. Works with the screen off, in a pocket.

Entrance 2 · Body

Fall + stillness

Opt-in: an impact followed by no movement starts a 20-second countdown the user can cancel; otherwise an SOS leaves with the motion evidence attached.

Entrance 3 · Voice

Bhasha SOS

Speak in Kannada; the medic reads Hindi. On-device transcription → a tiny structured packet that renders in the responder's language.

"ಅಮ್ಮ ಬಿದ್ದಿದ್ದಾರೆ, ಉಸಿರಾಡ್ತಿಲ್ಲ" → { type: MEDICAL, sev: CRITICAL, people: 1, sym: [collapse, NOT breathing], zone: Gate C, floor: +1 (barometer), conf: 0.82 } → medic (hi)

Built for negation

"Not breathing", "not injured" and "no longer blocked" must stay distinguishable. The evaluation set is built around negation, code-switching and vague locations; native speakers verify; real recordings and speakers stay separate between training and evaluation.

Order of operations

  1. Basic SOS leaves instantly — deterministic, CRITICAL
  2. Enrichment packets follow: category, severity, floor, language
  3. AI may raise priority or link reports; it never lowers, delays or merges away an explicit SOS
  4. False-trigger rate is measured and published per entrance
Accessibility is safety: the same entrances serve people who are deaf, non-verbal, injured, or simply holding a child with both hands.
Status: Bhasha SOS KEEP (two tested languages first). Pattern SOS NEXT. Fall + stillness RESEARCH → NEXT after false-alarm measurement on target phones.17

MeshMind11 / Research track
11 The ambitious bets

The moonshots —
with the maths stated up front.

SurgeWatch Research hypothesis

Could correlated motion anomalies at the front of a crowd give people further back useful lead time? Only if this inequality holds:

Twave > Tdetect + Tcorroborate + Tdeliver + Trespond

We have measured Tdelivery on phones over two hops: SOS p50 202 ms idle, 440 ms through a 1 MiB transfer. Crowd-disaster research supports studying stop-and-go and turbulent motion; it does not establish a warning lead time. So we measure each term separately, publish it, and never schedule "crush prediction" as a feature.

Scenario Rehearsal Simulation

"Gate C is reported blocked. Compare: leave instructions unchanged, ask staff to inspect Gate B, or use a pre-approved diversion." The tool evaluates options against a verified venue map, uncertain crowd estimates, route capacities, accessibility and evidence freshness — and says when the evidence is too thin to choose.

The compute problem nobody models

Couple crowd simulation to communication simulation. An instruction changes how people move; movement changes the mesh; the mesh changes who receives the next instruction. Our SAGE replay engine is the communication half of that loop.

MeshMind12 / Safety architecture
12 Safety by construction

AI proposes.
Deterministic rules dispose.

The more ambitious the AI, the harder the safety boundary has to be. MeshMind's boundary is not a policy document — it is code, and it is tested with a hostile model.

The adversarial test

We plug in a model that refuses every copy and crashes if it is ever shown an SOS. With 100% critical traffic its delivery, latency and transmissions are identical to flooding; in mixed traffic it scores thousands of ordinary rows and never sees one SOS. If the model is missing or corrupt, the hand-written heuristic takes over.

decide(packet, peer): if packet.priority == CRITICAL: return REPLICATE # before any model if peer is destination: return FORWARD try: score = sage(features) # ordinary only except: score = heuristic() # safe fallback # simulator re-applies the CRITICAL gate # AFTER the policy returns

SOS never waits for AICritical gate precedes model access; scheduler sends CRITICAL first; buffers never evict CRITICAL for ordinary traffic
No future leakageHistory state rejects time travel; a test rewrites the future of a trace and proves every earlier feature is unchanged
Shadow before authorityOn phones, SAGE first logs recommendations while the deterministic router decides; promotion needs a held-out win

MeshMind14 / Data + compute
14 The learning flywheel

Big compute in the lab.
Small, dependable models on the phone.

Programme 1

Failure laboratory

Replay real routing code against churn, gateway loss, uneven adoption, killed processes, bursty traffic. Model radio contention — perfect-link simulators lie.

Programme 2

SAGE vs strong baselines

Epidemic, Spray-and-Wait, Spray-and-Focus, PRoPHET and a same-mechanics PRoPHET-value policy. Tuned on validation, reported on held-out events.

Programme 3

Hard language set

Noise, code-switching, vague locations, negation. Native speakers verify; speakers never shared across train and test.

Programme 4

Distil everything

Expensive simulations become tiny models: which relay stays useful, which question matters, which reports are one incident — and when to abstain.

v2 target → v1 result (held-out simulation)

Target: −25% bytes at equal delivery, emergency delivery and p95 unchanged. Result: −28% total airtime at statistically equal delivery; SOS delivery and latency identical in all 484 runs. Not yet met: duplicate bytes fell only 12%. Next target: 3–4× less airtime at equal delivery (perfect-foresight bound: −72%), then the same on phones.

What we collect · and never collect

Consented, pseudonymous field telemetry: peer counts, contact durations, queue length, battery, packet size/type/TTL, relay choice, outcome, latency, hops. Never message text, media, phone numbers or persistent precise location.

Public training data used today: SocioPatterns (conference, exhibition, school, workplace, hospital) and the Copenhagen Networks Study. CRAWDAD traces require licence steps and are not used until obtained properly.22
